How it works

Three deterministic steps — no AI guessing

Governments spending public money must answer: who decided this, on what basis? OpenShelter can, line by line.

1

Hazard fingerprint

Your coordinates are looked up in gridded public datasets — USGS earthquakes, NOAA cyclone tracks today; national flood & heat layers next. Every value carries its source label.

2

Code profile

Each jurisdiction's rules (Eurocode 8, IS 139, ASCE 7, NSCP, Sphere) live in machine-readable JSON anyone can inspect, diff and correct via pull request — owned by the countries themselves.

3

Rule engine

Hazard × code produces ranked measures and compliance checks. Same inputs → byte-identical brief, forever. That is what makes it usable in audits and procurement.

Why "explainable" beats "smart"

A neural network cannot cite clause 4.2 of a national standard. Every OpenShelter recommendation prints the hazard value and the exact code clause that produced it. No hidden model, no proprietary data, no subscription.

What it deliberately does NOT do

It does not replace a licensed structural engineer, stamp drawings, or claim site-specific geotechnical truth. It closes the gap between "no plan at all" and "an unaffordable consultancy" by producing the pre-design brief that makes the engineer's job faster, cheaper and locally appropriate.

The problem in one paragraph

Billions of dollars flow into emergency shelters every year. Most are designed from copy-paste templates that ignore local codes and local hazards — so the same "safe" shelter that survives an earthquake in one country collapses in a flood somewhere else. Hazard maps exist. Building codes exist. Nothing free and auditable connected them — until now.

Who it serves

Built for the people who sign the checks — and the people who need the roof

🏛 Governments & disaster agencies

Turn shelter reconstruction budgets into code-compliant, auditable design requirements in minutes instead of months. Publish the briefs as open procurement documents aligned with the Sendai Framework.

🎓 Universities & schools

A real, forkable teaching tool for risk-engineering and policy studios. Students add their own country's code profile as coursework — and ship it upstream.

🤝 NGOs & community builders

Field teams get a printable checklist tuned to local hazards before buying materials. Runs offline on a laptop or phone; no account, no fees, nothing uploaded.

Get involved

Contribute — three ways, ten minutes each

  1. Add your country's building code. Run python tools/new_code_profile.py --id bd-bnbc --jurisdiction BD --name "Bangladesh National Building Code", fill the JSON, open a pull request. One file, fully validated by CI.
  2. Correct a number we got wrong. Every rule lives in plain JSON with citations. Fix it like a Wikipedia page; engineers worldwide benefit instantly.
  3. Translate the interface. Bengali, Spanish and French first — help a non-English-speaking municipality use it.
