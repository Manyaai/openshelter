Our reason for existing

Mission, vision & the story so far

A world where "we couldn't afford a safe design" is no longer a reason a shelter fails.

Our mission

Give every community, regardless of budget, the ability to design emergency shelters that respect their country's hazards and its own building codes, with every decision traceable to a public source.

Our vision

A world where "we couldn't afford a safe design" is no longer a reason a shelter fails. Public safety knowledge stays public: open data in, auditable guidance out, forever free for governments, NGOs and classrooms.

Why now

Climate displacement is rising, shelter budgets are under pressure, and digital public infrastructure is finally its own funding category (EU, UN, national DRR strategies). The gap between hazard science and a buildable checklist is exactly what an open project can close.
The best time to fix a shelter design is before the monsoon, not after the funeral.

The problem we refuse to accept

After every disaster, the same story repeats: temporary shelters built from generic templates, reused for years, failing in the next event because they were designed for the last one's hazard, or none at all. Satellite dashboards tell officials where floods happen. Thick code PDFs tell engineers how to build against them. Between those two documents sits a village waiting for a shelter that accounts for both.

No one owns that gap. Commercial risk software is proprietary and priced for insurers; government consultants produce one-off reports; open data sits unused. We built the bridge: a tool small enough to run offline on a field laptop, transparent enough to survive a procurement audit, and open enough that a standards body in Dhaka or Manila can own its own numbers without asking a vendor for permission.

The story so far

  • v0.1 · The foundation. Five machine-readable code profiles (Eurocode 8, IBC/ASCE 7, IS 139, NSCP, Sphere), a deterministic rule engine where every output cites its clause, a CLI that runs on stdlib-only Python, and full test coverage.
  • v0.2 · Real data + a public front door. USGS earthquake and NOAA cyclone climatology wired into both the Python core and a browser demo. A live multi-page website, brief gallery, governance docs and a funding map published.
  • Next · Country ownership. Flood and heat layers contributed by national agencies through pull requests they control, the first ones are why we're talking to disaster-management ministries today.

What we will never do

  1. Sell or gatekeep safety knowledge behind a paywall.
  2. Hide a recommendation behind a model that cannot cite a clause.
  3. Pretend to replace a licensed structural engineer, we make their work faster and cheaper, not optional.
  4. Ship a number without labelling where it came from, even when "demo placeholder" is an embarrassing answer.

Values

  • Auditable beats clever. Every output cites its inputs. No black boxes, ever.
  • Honest about limits. Demo data is labelled demo data; we say plainly when a licensed engineer is required.
  • Local ownership. Countries maintain their own code profiles through pull requests they control.
  • Boring technology. Plain text, JSON, git, deterministic rules, tools archivists and auditors love.

The road ahead

  • Now: real raster-backed flood & heat providers (GEM Risk Library, Copernicus), first pilot municipality, more code profiles.
  • Next: i18n (Bengali, Spanish, French), PDF export, a public registry of adopted briefs.
  • Later: an independent foundation holding the trademark and roadmap, funded the way Wikipedia and OSI fund open infrastructure.
