Mission, vision & the story so far

Our mission

Give every community — regardless of budget — the ability to design emergency shelters that respect their country's hazards and its own building codes, with every decision traceable to a public source.

Our vision

A world where "we couldn't afford a safe design" is no longer a reason a shelter fails. Public safety knowledge stays public: open data in, auditable guidance out, forever free for governments, NGOs and classrooms.

Why now

Climate displacement is rising, shelter budgets are under pressure, and digital public infrastructure is finally a funding category of its own (EU, UN, national DRR strategies). The gap between hazard science and a buildable checklist is exactly what an open project can close.
The best time to fix a shelter design is before the monsoon, not after the funeral.

The story

OpenShelter began with a simple observation: everyone maps disaster risk, almost nobody operationalises it. Satellite dashboards tell officials where floods happen; thick code PDFs tell engineers how to build against them; and between those two documents sits a village waiting for a shelter that accounts for both. We built the bridge first as a command-line tool small enough to run offline on a field laptop, then made every rule inspectable in plain JSON so a standards body in Dhaka or Manila can own its own numbers without asking permission from a vendor.

Version 0.1 shipped five code profiles and a deterministic rule engine. Version 0.2 wired real earthquake and cyclone data into both the Python core and the browser demo. The next chapters will be written by the agencies and universities who adopt it — that is by design.

Values

  • Auditable beats clever. Every output cites its inputs. No black boxes, ever.
  • Honest about limits. Demo data is labelled demo data; we say plainly when a licensed engineer is still required.
  • Local ownership. Countries maintain their own code profiles through pull requests they control.
  • Free as in foundation. AGPL, no accounts, no telemetry, runs offline — public money, public code.

Roadmap

  • v0.2 — now: real USGS + IBTrACS hazard grids in CLI and web demo · location presets · expanded site with mission page and honesty table.
  • v0.3 — next: national flood & heat layers via GEM/Copernicus providers · PDF export · Bengali/Spanish/French interface · pilot municipality case study.
  • v0.4: 25+ country code profiles · permissive-core dual licensing for sustainability · formal advisory board of national standards bodies.
  • Long-term: the Wikipedia of resilient construction rules — versioned, cited, and referenced in government procurement worldwide.

Outcomes we measure

We publish what matters to grant reviewers and taxpayers alike: number of jurisdictions with owned code profiles, briefs generated offline in the field, corrections contributed by national engineers, and (with pilots) cost and time saved per shelter design versus commercial consultancy. Our current baseline and goals live in GOVERNANCE.md and FUNDING.md.
